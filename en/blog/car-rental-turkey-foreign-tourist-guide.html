Renting a Car in Türkiye as a Foreign Tourist — 2026 Guide

Antalya, Bodrum, Istanbul or Cappadocia — the most flexible way to discover Türkiye is by rental car. But if it's your first time renting here, the paperwork, license recognition, and insurance choices can feel confusing. This guide pulls together everything a foreign tourist needs to know in 2026, in plain language and up to date.

Required Documents

To rent a car in Türkiye you need three things with you:

  • Passport — for identity verification.
  • Valid driving license — held for at least 1 year.
  • Credit card in the driver's name — for deposit and payment. Prepaid cards are generally not accepted.

Most rental companies require a minimum age of 21, or 25 for premium cars. Drivers aged 19-21 can rent with a "young driver" surcharge.

Is Your License Valid in Türkiye?

Good news: most common licenses are accepted as-is. European Union, United Kingdom and Russian Federation licenses are valid without translation. US, Canadian and Australian licenses are accepted for short-term use, but we recommend carrying an International Driving Permit (IDP) to be safe. For Gulf states, India, China and most of Asia, an IDP is effectively required.

💡 Tip: If your license is in a non-Latin script (Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese, etc.), an IDP or notarised translation will save time at the desk.

Traffic Rules — Quick Reference

  • Traffic drives on the right; steering on the left.
  • Speed limits: 50 km/h in town, 90 km/h outside town, 120 km/h on motorways.
  • Seatbelts are mandatory in all seats.
  • Blood-alcohol limit: 0.05% (roughly one small beer). Newly-licensed drivers: zero tolerance.
  • Handheld phone use is prohibited — Bluetooth/hands-free only.
  • Daytime running lights must be on year-round.

Driving in Antalya — What You Should Know

Antalya is a tourist-heavy region; in summer the beach roads to Lara and Konyaaltı can get congested. The D400 coastal road connects Antalya–Kemer–Side–Alanya — scenic, but some sections are winding.

Toll Roads (HGS)

The O-21 motorway from Antalya towards Alanya is tolled and uses HGS (Fast Pass System). Almost all rental cars come with HGS pre-installed; tolls are billed when you return the car. You do not need to buy a personal HGS sticker.

Fuel

Fuel prices in Türkiye are close to the European average. A hybrid saves significantly on long routes. Return the car at the same fuel level you received it — otherwise a refuelling + service fee will be charged.

Insurance, CDW and Deposit

Every rental car includes third-party liability insurance and mandatory traffic insurance. Comprehensive cover (CDW/casco) is optional and we strongly recommend it — without it, even the smallest scratch comes out of your pocket.

Typical packages:

  • Basic — liability only; no CDW.
  • Safe — CDW with ~2,500 TL excess (most popular choice).
  • All-Inclusive — zero excess, minor damage included.

A deposit between 4,000–22,000 TL is held on your credit card. This is a hold, not a charge; it's released 7-14 days after the car is returned.

Common Pitfalls — Avoid These

  • Photograph the car before driving off: every scratch, rim mark, bumper scuff — and have it noted on the handover form.
  • Don't return with an empty tank: return at the level you received.
  • Unauthorised driver: if someone not named on the contract drives, the insurance is void. Add an additional driver if needed.
  • Parking fines: prefer paid car parks or signed blue/green zones. Tickets arrive late and are charged to your card with a service fee.
